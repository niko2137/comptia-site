The formal change management workflow

The 9-Step Change Management Process

Change management is the formal process for requesting, evaluating, approving, implementing, and documenting changes to IT systems — it prevents unplanned outages caused by untested changes.

  • 1
    Documented Business Processes
    The formal reason and business justification for the change is recorded.
  • 2
    Purpose of the Change
    Clearly defines why the change is needed (new requirement, bug fix, security patch, etc.).
  • 3
    Scope of the Change
    Defines what systems, users, or processes will be affected.
  • 4
    Risk Analysis
    Evaluates potential risks and assigns a risk level (low/medium/high).
  • 5
    Plan for the Change
    Documents exactly how the change will be implemented, including a timeline.
  • 6
    End-User Acceptance
    Confirms affected users understand and accept the upcoming change.
  • 7
    Change Board Approval
    The CAB (Change Advisory Board) reviews and formally approves the change request.
  • 8
    Backout Plan
    Documents exactly how to revert the change if something goes wrong.
  • 9
    Document the Changes
    The change log is updated with the full record after implementation.

Additional Change Management Terms

Change Freeze

Defined period where non-emergency changes are prohibited (e.g., peak business season).

Peer Review

Another technician reviews a planned change before implementation to catch missed risks.

Standard Change

Low-risk, pre-approved, repeatable change (e.g., routine patching) — no full CAB review needed.

Normal Change

Follows the full change process, including CAB review, due to higher risk/complexity.

Emergency Change

Urgent change to resolve a critical issue, often bypassing normal approval; documented after the fact.

💡 Pro Tips

  • Network diagrams and documentation save enormous time during troubleshooting, especially in complex environments.
  • Change management prevents "it worked fine before" problems — always document changes.
  • Knowledge base articles are built from closed help desk tickets — good ticket documentation builds the KB.
  • Asset tags connect physical hardware to the inventory database: serial number + asset tag = full device history.

Hypervisors, VM resource allocation, snapshots vs. clones, and cloud service/deployment models for the CompTIA A+ 220-1200 exam

Type 1 vs. Type 2 hypervisor architecture

Hypervisor Types

FeatureType 1 (Bare Metal)Type 2 (Hosted)
ArchitectureRuns directly on hardware, no host OSRuns on top of a host OS
Best forEnterprise / server environments (most efficient)Students & labs (easiest to install)
ExamplesVMware ESXi, Hyper-V Server, Xen, KVMVMware Workstation, VirtualBox, Parallels Desktop

VDI (Virtual Desktop Infrastructure): hosts desktop OSes as VMs on a central server, streaming the desktop to thin clients — centralizes management since the OS/data never live on the endpoint.

Snapshots vs. Clones

FeatureSnapshotClone

📈 Elasticity vs. High Availability

  • Elasticity: automatic, on-demand scaling up or down in response to demand. All elastic systems are scalable; not all scalable systems are elastic (scalability can be manual).
  • High Availability (HA): minimal downtime through redundancy. 99.9% uptime ("three nines") = ~8.76 hours of downtime/year; 99.999% ("five nines") = ~5.26 minutes/year.

⚠️ Exam Traps

Snapshots are NOT backups — a snapshot lives on the same physical disk as the VM, so a disk failure destroys both. "The cloud provider is responsible for everything" is never correct — even in SaaS, the customer owns their data and compliance obligations (HIPAA, GDPR, etc.).
